\section{Mathematical changes}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Correct the domain and formula of the Kubota compact splitting: for integral matrices the relevant nonunit condition is $c\in\varpi\mathcal O_F$, not $c\notin\mathcal O_F$. The genuine inducing type uses the nontrivial character of the kernel, not the trivial representation.
\item Correct the location of the lift of the determinant-$\varpi$ matrix: it lies in the covering group of $\mathrm{GL}_2(F)$.
\item Reorient the Iwahori to match the chosen adjacent vertex. The original displayed pro-$p$ Iwahori and the original intersection definition were not consistent as written.
\item Replace unsupported global pointwise character statements by a finite regular elliptic trace formula. On the split torus the original grouped-convergence argument is not established in this draft.
\item Preserve the original same-inducing-type elliptic comparison and its compact-induction method. Extend it to a weighted two-orbit comparison with explicit quadratic edge discrepancy. The edge calculation, path product, constant parity factor, fixed-ball proof, depth-zero closed formulas and signed sum are additions in this revision. The finite-group character values, character existence theorem and admissibility of standard depth-zero inductions are cited inputs, not new theorems.
\item The general elliptic comparison for higher-level inducing types is conditional on admissibility of both inductions. No exhaustive identification of Manderscheid's parameters or positive-depth classification has been undertaken.
\item Do not extrapolate the odd-degree inverse-power identity to squaring. Distinguish the direct comparison from theta correspondence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical changes from Draft 1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Edge discrepancy (Proposition 3.8, was 3.2).} The statement is unchanged and correct. The proof is new and self-contained. It shows that the Kubota cocycle is trivial on upper and on lower unipotent pairs (Lemma 3.5), that every canonical splitting sends a unipotent $n$ to $(n,1)$, and it computes $\widetilde w(t)\widetilde w(t')^{-1}=(h(t/t'),(-t',t)_2)$ (Lemma 3.6). Writing $h(u)=w(\varpi u)w(\varpi)^{-1}$ inside $K_1$ gives $s_1(h(u))=(h(u),(\varpi,u)_2)$. The Draft 1 proof through the extended cocycle $\beta'$ on $\mathrm{GL}_2(F)$ is kept as Remark 3.9. Its two displayed values of $\beta'$ were rechecked by hand and are correct. The statement for an arbitrary edge is now intrinsic: the sign is $\ell$ of the residue eigenvalue of Lemma 2.1. Draft 1 said only that the formula holds ``in transported coordinates''.
\item \textbf{Uniqueness of splittings (Lemma 3.3, was 3.1).} Same statement. The proof now uses that $K_i$ is generated by unipotent elements, each of which is a square. The continuity hypothesis is no longer needed. A short paragraph after the lemma records that a homomorphism from a pro-$p$ group to $\mu_2$ is trivial without continuity, and that each $s_v$ is continuous.
\item \textbf{Fixed sets (Lemma 2.3, new).} Finiteness of the fixed set of a regular elliptic element is now proved by orders in $F[t]$. Draft 1 proved it inside Theorem 4.1 by an argument with ends of the tree, which was correct in outline but left the passage from a fixed ray to a fixed line as a sketch.
\item \textbf{Vertex model and block traces (Lemmas 2.5 and 2.7, new).} These make explicit the steps that Draft 1 described in one paragraph of the proof of Theorem 4.1.
\item \textbf{Weighted fixed-vertex formula (Theorem 4.2, was 4.1).} Same formulas. The neighborhood on which the fixed set is constant is now defined explicitly. \emph{Change of status of an input:} Draft 1 used the existence and local constancy of character functions (Harish-Chandra, Li) as cited input. Draft 2 does not need it on the regular elliptic set, because the character function is exhibited. Admissibility of the two inductions remains a hypothesis.
\item \textbf{Direct comparison (Corollary 4.5, was 4.3).} Same statement, now in three parts. Added: the sign is $-1$ exactly when $j\ne i$, $z=-1$ and $q\equiv3\pmod 4$. Added Remark 4.6: the lift $s_w(t)$ depends only on the type of $w$, and the genuine character at the lift of the inducing type always equals the ordinary character.
\item \textbf{Lemma 4.4 (was 4.2).} Now also states that the same $z$ occurs on every fixed edge, which Corollary 4.5 uses.
\item \textbf{Depth-zero formulas (Proposition 5.1, Theorem 5.4, Corollary 5.6; were 5.1, 5.2, 5.3).} Unchanged. Proofs expanded. The finite character value on split semisimple elements was added to the list of cited values, for use in Proposition 7.3.
\item \textbf{Diagonal conjugation (Proposition 7.1).} This was an inline claim in Section 6 of Draft 1, stated for $h(u)\in G$. It is now a proposition with a complete proof, valid for all $u\in F^\times$.
\item \textbf{Vanishing at split elements with regular reduction (Proposition 7.3, new).} For generic depth-zero types, both distribution characters vanish on $h(u)K_0^+$ when $\bar u\ne\pm1$. This follows from the block-trace formula and the vanishing of the finite cuspidal character on split regular elements. Remark 2026.1 is narrowed accordingly to split elements near the center and to positive depth.
\item \textbf{Unramified tori (Section 2.5).} Draft 1 justified the non-conjugacy of $T_0$ and $T_1$ in $G$ by the determinants of the centralizer. The argument needs the normalizer, and it is now stated that way.
\item \textbf{Compact induction (Section 2.3).} Draft 1 said the support is a finite union of left cosets of $H$. With $f(hg)=\sigma(h)f(g)$ the support is a union of cosets $Hg$. Corrected.
\item \textbf{Section 5.4.} The statement that $\pi_{0,\theta}\oplus\pi_{1,\theta}$ is the restriction of a depth-zero supercuspidal of $\mathrm{GL}_2(F)$ is now marked as a standard fact recalled for orientation and not used.
\item \textbf{Table of Section 6.} The Draft 1 table declared six columns and filled five. A column for the signed sum was added. All entries were recomputed.
\end{enumerate}
No statement of Draft 1 was found to be false.

\section{Response to Carbon's referee report on Draft 1}\label{sec:carbon}
Carbon found the core mathematics sound and listed clarifications. Each point was checked independently. Statement numbers are those of Draft 2.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Multiplication law for the extended cocycle.} Agreed. The law on $\mathrm{GL}_2(F)\times\mu_2$ is now displayed in Section 3.3. Remark 3.9 now explains that $\widetilde D(h(u),1)$ and $(h(u),1)\widetilde D$ differ by $\beta'(D,h(u))\beta'(h(u),D)$ because $D$ and $h(u)$ commute, so the inverse of $\widetilde D$ is not needed.
\item \emph{Description of $I^+$.} Agreed. $I^+$ is now defined in coordinates in Example 2.2, with $a\equiv d\equiv1$ and $b\in\varpi\mathcal O_F$.
\item \emph{Haar measure in Theorem 4.2.} Agreed in substance. The proof already used that every $x\in U$ has the same fixed set. Two sentences now state that the same Haar measure appears on both sides, for $G$ and for the cover. No volume of $U$ enters, since the identity is between two integrals over $U$.
\item \emph{Inflation to $K_1$ and outer automorphisms.} Agreed, with one correction to the report. For $\theta^2\ne1$ the representation $\sigma_\theta$ is irreducible, so there are no two components $\sigma_\theta^\pm$ to exchange. The relevant point is that an automorphism from $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)$ can exchange the two unipotent classes, and $\chi_\theta$ takes the same value on both. Section 5.2 now says this and notes that the case $\theta^2=1$ is different.
\item \emph{Ramified elements.} Agreed. Section 8.2 now gives the reason that $\bar\tau=\pm1$.
\item \emph{$K_i^+$ and $K_0(m)$.} Agreed. $K_v^+$ is defined in Section 2.1, $K_0^{(m)}$ is in the notation table, and $K_0^{(1)}=K_0^+$ is stated.
\item \emph{$\mu_2$.} Agreed. It is defined in the introduction and in the notation table.
\item \emph{Remark numbering.} The clumsy double label is removed. The labels ``Remark 2026.$n$'' are kept on their own counter, as rule 6.7 of the style guide requires for statements not fully verified. Carbon's proposed plain titles are used as the remark titles.
\item \emph{Exponent $j-i$.} A sentence after Lemma 4.4 states that only the parity of $j-i$ matters.
\item \emph{Glossary.} The entry on packet sums is restored with a mathematical statement in place of the phrase Carbon objected to. The entries for \emph{lattice} and \emph{central extension} are expanded. Carbon's suggested wording that central extensions are ``classified'' by symbol cocycles was not used, since the paper does not cite a classification theorem.
\end{enumerate}
Carbon's verdict that the edge discrepancy, the weighted sum, the parity factor, the fixed-ball radius and the numerical examples are correct agrees with the independent checks of Section~\ref{sec:verif}.
