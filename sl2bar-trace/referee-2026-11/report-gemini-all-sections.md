* **Section 7.4 (`sec7-stable.tex`), Proposition 7.8$'$ (`propprime`), Part (b):**
* *Issue:* The uniqueness proof for $h_\pi(z)$ says: "If $h_1$ is a second such function, $h_1/h_\pi$ has no zero or pole in $\vert{}z\vert{}<1$ and absolute value $1$ on the circle, hence by reflection no zero or pole at all". Because $P(z)$ can have even-order zeros or poles *on* the unit circle $\vert{}z\vert{}=1$ (which enter $h(z)$ via $\prod_{\vert{}\alpha\vert{}=1}(1-z/\alpha)^{m_\alpha/2}$), an arbitrary rational $h_1$ assumed only regular/nonvanishing on $\vert{}z\vert{}<1$ could a priori have poles or zeros on $\vert{}z\vert{}=1$ unless boundary regularity is specified or canceled.


* *Fix:* Either state in Proposition 7.8$'$(b) that $h_\pi(z)$ is required to have no poles on $\vert{}z\vert{}\le 1$ (except where $p(\pi,\lambda)$ has poles) — or, simpler, note that $\vert{}h_1(z)\vert{}^2 = \vert{}h_\pi(z)\vert{}^2 = P(z)$ on $\vert{}z\vert{}=1$ forces $h_1$ and $h_\pi$ to have the exact same divisor of zeros and poles on $\vert{}z\vert{}=1$ (each of half the order of $P$), so $h_1/h_\pi$ is regular and nonvanishing on $\vert{}z\vert{}=1$ as well, making reflection across $\vert{}z\vert{}=1$ immediate.
